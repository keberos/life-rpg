A. Classic 16-bit

SNES-era JRPG

Chunky pixels, black outlines, bright saturated colors, blue menu windows with white borders. The most nostalgic look; small details are bold but simple.

B. Hi-bit, lit

Modern pixel art

Pixels at double density with soft lighting, colored outlines and a glow behind the cast, plus a clean fantasy UI with gold trim and readable type. Closest to "Final Fantasy, but modern."

C. Soft pastel

Cozy pixel

Rounded "plush" pixels, pastel colors and soft outlines. Friendly and calm, more cozy game than epic quest.

D. Handheld 4-tone

Retro handheld

Everything in four shades of green, like an old handheld screen. Very distinctive and cheap to make, but items are harder to tell apart without color.

E. Flat illustrated

Not pixel art

Smooth vector shapes and a bright, modern app look. For contrast: easiest to read at any size, but it leaves the pixel-RPG feel behind.
